How the simulation works

Each lap adds reference pace, a compound offset and tyre-age degradation, then subtracts a fuel effect. Stops add pit loss and reset tyre age.

Compound offsets: S 0.00, M +0.45, H +0.90 seconds. Wear factors: S ×1.35, M ×0.85, H ×0.55.

These are illustrative coefficients, not fitted tyre physics. No traffic, overtaking, tyre inventory or sporting-rule validation is modelled. Dry compounds are assumed available.

What these scores mean

Models predict an observed opening sequence, finish among classified drivers, or pit-filtered average pace. They do not identify the optimal strategy. The simulator above is a separate, transparent mathematical model.

Where support runs out

Only three historical seasons are available. Tyre labels and regulations change. Race weather is observed retrospectively; conditioning on it is a scenario, not a weather forecast. Rare and unseen tyre sequences remain difficult.

Per-race test results and uncertainty

Intervals resample event-level mean errors or accuracies across 1,000 bootstrap draws. They describe variation within this small historical sample, not individual-driver prediction intervals.
